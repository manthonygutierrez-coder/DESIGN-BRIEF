HomeLettering

Headstones, kerbs and churchyard repairs for the eleven parishes between Broughton and Coniston. Every letter on every stone that has left this yard since 1904 was cut here, by somebody whose name is in the wages book. Until August they were drawn freehand on the stone and cut straight off the drawing.

Lettering, from 1 January

The Chancellor’s revised churchyard regulations take effect on 1 January and no longer permit applied lead or plastic letters on any new stone in this diocese. Everything leaving the yard after that date is incised on the banker or blasted through rubber cut here. Stones already standing are unaffected.

How a stone is made here

Stone kept
Burlington blue-grey slate, Lazonby red sandstone, Portland limestone
Lettering
Incised on the banker, or blasted through rubber cut on the premises
Letter height
Capitals 40 to 75 mm. The regulations do not permit more
Finish
Rubbed, not polished. Six of the eleven churchyards forbid a polished face
Fixing
Dowelled into a 450 mm foundation, by us, on a dry day
From order
Fourteen to twenty weeks. Frost stops the fixing, not the cutting
